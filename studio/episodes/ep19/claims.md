# Ep 19 claims + sources: "Is 'Hangry' Real? What Hunger Does to Your Mood"
Trend/timing context: doctors warning about extreme fasting windows and viral wellness trends (inkl, Nov 2026). This is NOT a diet video and nothing encourages skipping meals.

| # | Claim in the video | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "64 people logged their mood 9,000 times." | Swami V et al., Hangry in the field, PLoS One 2022: 64 participants, 21-day experience sampling, 5 time-points/day, total 9,142 responses. PMID 35793289. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0269629 | VERIFIED in abstract. "9,000" is rounded from 9,142 (description says 9,142). |
| 2 | "Hungrier meant angrier and more irritable." | Same abstract: greater hunger went with greater anger and irritability, and lower pleasure; held after adjusting for sex, age, BMI, dietary behaviours, trait anger. Arousal NOT significant. | VERIFIED. Observational association. |
| 3 | "In a trial, fasted women reported more anger and tension." | Ackermans MA et al., Hunger increases negative and decreases positive emotions in women with a healthy weight, Appetite 2021: randomized, fasted (n=53) vs ate breakfast (n=55); hungry women reported higher tension, anger, fatigue and confusion. PMID 34637770. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.appet.2021.105746 | VERIFIED. Women with healthy BMI only; the fasting length is deliberately NOT on screen or in the script. |
| 4 | "That's a link, not the full why." | Neither paper tests a mechanism. Swami is observational. | VERIFIED honest framing. |

## What we do NOT say (dropped from the pitch)
- The pitched hook "your brain runs on sugar, so why angry not sleepy?" is NOT used: Ackermans found fasted women reported MORE fatigue too, and neither paper shows a blood-sugar mechanism.
- No fasting hours, no diet targets, no "skip a meal" advice. Description adds "if worries about food or eating are weighing on you, talk to your doctor".
- Study populations: Swami = 64 adults in Central Europe; Ackermans = women only. Said in the description.
