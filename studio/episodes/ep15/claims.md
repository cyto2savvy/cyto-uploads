# Ep 15 claims and sources: "Can exercise actually lower your cancer risk?"
All figures below were read from the PubMed records (PMIDs 40450658, 27183032, 42218651) on Nov 9, 2026. Neither main source is used in any earlier Short (`python3 studio/check_sources.py` = OK).

| # | Line in the video | Claim, exactly as supported | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "In a Canadian-led trial..." | Phase 3 randomized trial (CHALLENGE, CCTG CO.21), 55 centres, funded by the Canadian Cancer Society and others; led by Canadian investigators with Australian and UK sites | Courneya 2025, NEJM, doi 10.1056/NEJMoa2502760, PMID 40450658 |
| 2 | "exercise cut colon cancer's comeback risk by about 28 percent" | Disease-free survival HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.55-0.94, P=0.02) = 28% lower hazard of recurrence, new primary cancer or death (a composite, primary endpoint) | same |
| 3 | "colon cancer survivors did a three-year exercise plan after chemo" | 889 patients with resected stage III or high-risk stage II colon cancer who had finished adjuvant chemo; structured exercise program for 3 years vs health-education materials | same |
| 4 | "Five years on, 80 percent were disease-free, versus 74 percent with pamphlets" | 5-year disease-free survival 80.3% vs 73.9% (difference 6.4 points, 95% CI 0.6-12.2) | same |
| 5 | "Across 1.44 million adults" | 12 pooled prospective US and European cohorts, 1.44 million participants, 186,932 cancers; self-reported leisure-time activity | Moore 2016, JAMA Intern Med, doi 10.1001/jamainternmed.2016.1548, PMID 27183032 |
| 6 | "the most active had lower risk of 13 cancers" | High vs low activity (90th vs 10th percentile) associated with lower risk of 13 of 26 cancers (e.g. lung HR 0.74, colon 0.84, breast 0.90) | same |
| 7 | "Linked, not proven" | Observational cohorts; association, not causation. 10 of 13 inverse associations stayed significant after BMI adjustment | same |
| 8 | "So hop off the TTC early and walk" | Practical nudge only, no numeric claim. Moore: association was similar in people with and without overweight and across smoking status for most cancers | same |

## Caveats that MUST be on screen or in the description/pinned comment
- The trial was in colon cancer PATIENTS after chemo, not healthy people. The video says "survivors... after chemo" and "not just for patients" and moves to the observational data for everyone else.
- The 28% is a hazard ratio on a composite (recurrence, new primary cancer or death). The script says "comeback risk"; the description spells out the composite.
- Overall survival (HR 0.63) was reported as "consistent with" a benefit, a secondary finding, so it is NOT in the script.
- Exercise group had more musculoskeletal adverse events (18.5% vs 11.5%). In the description.
- Moore 2016: melanoma (HR 1.27) and prostate (HR 1.05) risk were HIGHER with more activity. In the description so we are not cherry-picking. The video says "lower risk of 13 cancers", which is true as stated.
- "Not medical advice" (spoken + description + pinned comment). Description tells cancer patients to check with their care team before starting an exercise plan.

## Pinned-comment extra (not in the video)
Chan et al., J Clin Oncol 2026 (PMID 42218651, doi 10.1200/JCO-26-00765): cost-utility analysis of CHALLENGE from the Canadian public-payer view; the program cost about $2,917 CAD per person up front but was cost-saving overall (-$1,589 per person, +0.10 QALYs).
