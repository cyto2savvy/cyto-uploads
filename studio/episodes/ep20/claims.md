# Ep 20 claims + sources: "Why Do Freezing Fingers Suddenly Warm Up?"
Angle changed from the pitched "why does rewarming hurt": no PubMed source for the rewarming-pain mechanism was found, so this episode covers cold-induced vasodilation (CIVD), which is sourced.
Timing: first real cold of the season in Toronto.

| # | Claim in the video | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Cold squeezes finger blood vessels, so skin chills and hands get clumsy." | Seeley AD et al., Am J Physiol Regul Integr Comp Physiol 2026: "Cold-induced peripheral vasoconstriction reduces skin blood flow, skin temperatures, and manual dexterity." PMID 41870602. https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpregu.00176.2025 | VERIFIED in abstract. |
| 2 | "Then they reopen: cold-induced vasodilation." | Weller RS et al., Eur J Appl Physiol 2023 (also Weller, Wilderness Environ Med 2024): CIVD = "blood vessel dilation in response to cold exposure"; keyword "hunting reaction". PMID 37864008. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-023-05304-2 | VERIFIED. |
| 3 | "Ten people held a hand in ice-cold water. Fingers rebounded." | Weller 2023: 10 adults (8 men, 2 women), two 30-min hand immersions in 4.3 +/- 0.9 C water; CIVD onset time and finger temperatures measured per finger. | VERIFIED. "Rebounded" = the CIVD response measured. Small lab study; description says so. |
| 4 | "Gloves at the Bathurst stop beat hoping." | Common sense / flavour. The studies do not test gloves. | Flavour; description states warm-up is not a reason to skip gloves. |

## Caveats on screen / in description
- Small lab studies on healthy adults, mostly young men.
- No temperatures on screen (dial and curve are illustrative, no numerals).
- The pitched claim "rewarming hurts more than freezing" is NOT made: no source.
- Description: fingers that stay white, numb or painful after warming up -> see a doctor (Seeley/Weller context; frostbite protocol paper PMID 42384943 notes lasting cold intolerance and chronic pain after frostbite, not used in the video).
